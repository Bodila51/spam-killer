# Asia-Pacific: letters and do-not-call, by country of residence

Researched 2026-10-27 on official sources. Re-open the official page before relying on a detail; flows change.

## Australia

- **Law:** Privacy Act 1988, Australian Privacy Principles (APPs). Many small businesses (under A$3m turnover) are exempt.
- **Rights:** APP 7.6: ask an organisation to stop sending you direct marketing, to stop using your information to help
  others market to you, and to tell you the **source** of your information. No charge (APP 7.7). APP 12: access.
  APP 11.2: information no longer needed must be destroyed or de-identified. There is **no general right to erasure**, so
  the letter asks, it does not demand.
- **Deadline:** "within a reasonable period" for organisations (no fixed number of days).
- **Regulator:** OAIC: https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-complaints (complain to the organisation first).
- **Do-not-call:** Do Not Call Register (ACMA): https://www.donotcall.gov.au, or 1300 792 958. Personal numbers; permanent;
  takes 30 days. Does not cover scam calls or texts.
- **Letter:**
  > To {{COMPANY}}: I am {{NAME}} (phone {{PHONE}}, email {{EMAIL}}). Under APP 7.6 please stop using my personal
  > information for direct marketing and stop disclosing it to others for their marketing, and tell me the source of my
  > personal information. Under APP 12 please give me access to the information you hold. If you no longer need it for a
  > permitted purpose, please destroy or de-identify it (APP 11.2). Please act within a reasonable period.

## New Zealand

- **Law:** Privacy Act 2020, Information Privacy Principles (IPPs).
- **Rights:** IPP 6 access; IPP 7 correction. There is **no general right to erasure** and no Privacy Act marketing opt-out;
  ask the company to stop marketing and to remove data it no longer needs.
- **Deadline:** 20 working days for access requests.
- **Regulator:** Office of the Privacy Commissioner, complaint form: https://www.privacy.org.nz/your-privacy/complaint-form
  (complain to the agency first).
- **Do-not-call:** no government registry. Use the generic letter and the company's unsubscribe option.

## Singapore

- **Law:** Personal Data Protection Act 2012 (PDPA).
- **Rights:** withdraw consent (s 16, on reasonable notice); access to your data and how it was used or disclosed in the past
  year (s 21); correction (s 22). No free-standing right to erasure.
- **Regulator:** PDPC: https://www.pdpc.gov.sg (complaints need Singpass: the user's step).
- **Do-not-call:** DNC Registry: https://www.dnc.gov.sg — online, or SMS "DNC" to 78771 from the number. Takes up to 21 days.
  Does not cover scams. Scammers impersonate PDPC officers.
- **Letter:**
  > To {{COMPANY}}: I am {{NAME}}. Under s 16 of the PDPA I withdraw consent to your collection, use and disclosure of my
  > personal data, including phone {{PHONE}} and email {{EMAIL}}, and ask you to stop all marketing messages to me. Under
  > s 21 please give me access to my personal data and how it was used or disclosed in the past year, and tell me where it
  > came from if you can. Please confirm in writing.

## South Korea

- **Law:** Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA).
- **Rights:** Art. 35 access; Art. 36 correction or deletion (unless another law requires keeping it); Art. 37 suspension of
  processing / withdrawal of consent; Art. 20: a controller that got your data from someone else must, on request, tell you
  the **source**, the purpose and your right to demand suspension.
- **Deadline:** the controller must notify you of the result of a correction or deletion request within 10 days.
- **Regulator:** Personal Information Protection Commission: https://www.pipc.go.kr/eng
- **Letter:**
  > To {{COMPANY}}: Under PIPA I, {{NAME}}, request: (1) access under Art. 35 to my personal information, including phone
  > {{PHONE}} and email {{EMAIL}}; (2) under Art. 20, the source from which you obtained it; (3) under Art. 36 its deletion,
  > and under Art. 37 suspension of processing and withdrawal of all consent, including for marketing. Please notify me of
  > the outcome within 10 days.

## Japan

- **Law:** Act on the Protection of Personal Information (APPI); English text on japaneselawtranslation.go.jp.
- **Rights:** Art. 33 disclosure of retained personal data; Art. 35(1) and (5) cease use and delete (when handled unlawfully,
  no longer needed, or likely to harm your rights); Art. 35(3) stop providing to third parties. No explicit
  direct-marketing right and no clear "source" right: the letter can still ask how the data was obtained.
- **Deadline:** "without delay" (no number of days). The business may set its own request procedure and charge a reasonable
  fee for disclosure (Art. 37, 38).
- **Regulator:** Personal Information Protection Commission (PPC): https://www.ppc.go.jp/en/contactus/piinquiry/
- **Do-not-call:** none in Japan. Refuse on the call; the seller may not call again after a refusal.
- **Letter:** preferably in Japanese:
  > {{COMPANY}} 御中: 私、{{NAME}}（電話 {{PHONE}}、メール {{EMAIL}}）は、個人情報保護法第33条に基づき、貴社が保有する
  > 私の保有個人データの開示を請求します。また、マーケティング目的での利用に同意しておらず、利用の必要もないため、
  > 第35条第1項及び第5項に基づく利用停止及び消去、並びに第35条第3項に基づく第三者提供の停止を請求します。
  > 当該データの取得方法及び取得元もお知らせください。遅滞なくご対応ください。

## India

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and its Rules (notified November 2025) are being phased in over 18 months
(per the government's PIB release). The individual rights (access, correction, erasure, withdrawal of consent) are
**not yet enforceable** as of October 2026; once in force, companies must answer within 90 days. Until then: send the
generic letter (`templates/letter-generic.md`), withdraw consent, and state no legal deadline.
- **Do-not-call:** TRAI's DND (National Customer Preference Register): call or SMS **1909** from your number, or use the
  TRAI DND app. Information: https://trai.gov.in . It does not stop scam calls from unregistered senders.
